# Clock Primer — For the Comparison Desk

You do not need prior astronomy knowledge.

## Three layers

- **Right ascension (R.A.)** identifies the east-west position of the observed field.
- **Hour angle (H.A.)** describes how far that field lies from the local meridian.
- **Local sidereal time (L.S.T.)** is the reading that should agree with their sum.

For the convention used in these records:

`L.S.T. = R.A. + H.A.`

### Unrelated example

`04 10 00.0 + 00 20 00.0 W = 04 30 00.0`

Treat minutes and seconds like ordinary clock arithmetic: sixty seconds make one minute; sixty minutes make one hour.

## Clock corrections

If a standard is described as “fast,” its raw reading is ahead. Correct it by subtracting the measured error—or by adding that error with a negative sign.

For an unrelated example, a standard that is fast by `42.0 seconds` takes a signed correction of `−42.0 seconds`. Replacing `−42.0` with `+42.0` places the two resulting answers `84.0 seconds` apart.

S-2 records **sidereal** seconds. A civil chronometer records **mean-solar** seconds. Do not add a sidereal interval directly to a civil clock reading. Gatehouse Card L-2 supplies the in-world conversion rule and the paired civil/sidereal reading needed for that final step.

## What to compare

First establish that the records concern the same plate. Then compare the L.S.T. required by the plate field with the raw S-2 midpoint. An in-world rate record must supply the measured correction, and Card L-2 must supply the civil tie; this primer supplies only the conventions. The plate does not print a civil-time label—it contributes an independent astronomical relationship.
