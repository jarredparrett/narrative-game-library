# Thomas Rook

**Instrument Engineer · Dome and Clock Works**

## What everyone knows

You maintain the telescope drive, shutters, generator, and precision clocks. When an astronomer dislikes a result, the machinery is the first accused.

## What only you know

You made an unauthorized adjustment to the clock contact and backdated the maintenance entry. The repair introduced a small rate error amounting to less than one second during the reunion. It was negligent and embarrassing, but it cannot explain why a measured `−56.8 second` correction appears elsewhere as `+56.8 seconds`.

Your service plan shows a disused clock-weight and drive chase beside the solid pier. Mercer requested the service key earlier in the evening, claiming he needed to inspect an endowment-funded repair.

## Your objectives

1. Admit and quantify your repair error before it becomes a convenient murder theory.
2. Demonstrate the difference between accumulated drift and a sudden written correction.
3. Account for the service key and inspect the mounting route with host authorization.
4. Do not let technical language become a barrier; translate every conclusion into ordinary terms.

## Your opening belief

The blackout may have trapped or injured Alistair inside the dome machinery, but the maintenance route must be inspected before that theory survives.

## Your first move

Offer to draw the clock chain: master time, written correction, drive contact, and exposure entry. Ask who benefited from confusing those layers.
