# Felix Mercer

**Trustee and Treasurer · Vale Observatory**

## What everyone knows

You control the endowment accounts and have repeatedly warned that the observatory cannot survive another year of improvised management. Alistair trusted your financial discipline even when he despised your caution.

## What only you know

You diverted restricted endowment securities to support a private obligation, intending to restore them after the winter appeal. Alistair discovered the transfer. He summoned you to the plate vault and said his confession would force a complete audit.

During the confrontation you struck him with the heavy clock-winding handle. He died. You moved him through the maintenance passage below the mounting and concealed him in the disused clock-weight and drive chase beside the solid pier. Then you reversed the sign on the sidereal-standard correction sheet. Applying `+56.8 sidereal seconds` instead of `−56.8 sidereal seconds` shifted the reduction by 113.6 sidereal seconds, or 113.3 mean-solar seconds on Gatehouse Card L-2, and placed the false close inside your `10:04 P.M.` library entry.

You are the culprit. You may misdirect, bargain, omit, and advance plausible theories. You may not alter, destroy, or indefinitely withhold a resource after the host releases it, and you may not contradict a fact printed in your own materials.

## Your objectives

1. Keep the group focused on Rook's admitted clock fault or Ruth's grievance.
2. Prevent the final plate from being compared with both the correction sheet and the telescope-position record.
3. Explain the transfer schedule without admitting diversion.
4. If the case becomes conclusive, choose whether to confess, blame institutional necessity, or force the group to state the proof aloud.

## Your opening belief

The final plate cannot identify your alteration unless the group links its field to the observing log, chronograph contacts, and correction sheet.

## Your first move

Offer to organize a formal timeline. Put Rook's clock repair at its center.
