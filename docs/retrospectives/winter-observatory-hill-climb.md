# Winter Observatory hill-climb retrospective

**Retrospective date:** 2026-08-06

**Current gameplay baseline:** Candidate 6

**Current artifact-realism standing:** not accepted

## Executive summary

The climb produced a coherent, deterministic, no-hint-solvable game package
with strong critical-accessibility parity. It did not produce artifact-realism
acceptance. Those are separate results, and the most important success of the
process is that the records let us say both at once.

Fresh blind players found structural defects that a successful accusation
alone would have hidden: the evidence did not initially prove death, reading
copies were not evidentially equivalent to PDFs, correction authorship was
ambiguous, a referenced time-service card did not exist, and the final release
concentrated too much proof at once. Candidate 6 repaired those defects and
passed every frozen development-game gate with two independent 94%-confidence
accusations and no hints.

The document-realism climb improved the suite but remained far below its frozen
acceptance threshold. Candidate 3 averaged 10.1 across 18 native artifacts;
Candidate 6 averaged 16.7 across 19. No individual artifact was accepted in
either suite. Candidate 7's bounded rendering preflights showed development
movement, but Candidate 7 was never sealed, replayed, human-approved, or
independently measured.

Issue #18 converts this once-fragmented trace into a portable game Experiment.
The exact Candidate 6 Release, Physical Export, collection, approval, gameplay
manifest, and all 19 public Verismill replays now survive in one `.ngw`. This
migration creates neither a new Candidate nor new standing.

## What worked

### Hard gates overruled attractive scores

Candidates 4 and 5 produced correct no-hint accusations and good aggregate
experience scores. They were still rejected because critical accessibility and
released-evidence gates failed. A mystery is not fair merely because a capable
player can guess the intended answer.

The useful loop was:

```text
successful outcome
  -> inspect whether released evidence licensed it
  -> preserve quoted defects as requirements
  -> build a child
  -> replay with fresh isolated players
```

### Canonical facts and one explicit defect held the world together

The stable canon and correction-sign defect kept the mystery coherent across
revisions. Players repeatedly reproduced the same plate identity, `−56.8`
honest correction, `+56.8` posted correction, `113.6` sidereal-second
separation, motive, key interval, and concealment route. Repairs changed
evidentiary support without creating ambient contradictions.

### The unit of climb became the individual artifact

Moving away from one combined PDF enabled independent hashes and attestations,
byte-identical carry-forward, targeted regeneration, artifact-specific source
contracts, and separate physical and accessible renditions. Candidate 6's
lineage is particularly clear: 17 artifacts were carried byte-identically, one
was regenerated, and one was explicitly new.

### Gameplay and artifact panels measured different truths

Artifact panels measured whether a page looked and behaved like a credible
1937 record. Blind play measured whether a person could use the collection to
reach a licensed conclusion. Neither instrument replaced the other.

The highest-value gameplay findings were precise proof failures:

- “recovered” did not prove death;
- “Prepared: Rook / Checked: Mercer” did not establish who entered the sign;
- a sidereal interval could not simply be applied to civil time;
- Card L-2 could not be load-bearing while absent;
- a reading copy could neither omit visible evidence nor add interpretation.

### Human approval was scoped to exact bytes

Approval named the collection manifest, Candidate, scope, and limitations. It
authorized independent measurement but explicitly did not create realism
standing. The new Experiment spine preserves those original bytes and rejects
a Candidate or collection mismatch.

## What struggled

### Solvability matured much faster than artifact realism

| Artifact dimension | Candidate 3 | Candidate 6 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Mean artifact overall | 10.1 | 16.7 | +6.6 |
| Cross-field consistency | 45.7 | 69.7 | +24.0 |
| Drafting realism | 27.2 | 46.1 | +18.9 |
| External verifiability | 12.7 | 28.2 | +15.5 |
| Financial/operational | 27.0 | 46.4 | +19.4 |
| Forensic authenticity | 18.2 | 21.9 | +3.7 |
| Procedural correctness | 24.3 | 41.4 | +17.1 |
| Visual formatting | 44.3 | 56.9 | +12.6 |
| Accepted artifacts | 0/18 | 0/19 | none |

The frozen artifact gate required every artifact's worst judge score to be at
least 80. Candidate 6 minima ranged from 2 to 20. The bottleneck was structural:
capture geometry, paper behavior, handwriting, source-calibrated form anatomy,
and ordinary institutional record behavior. Rewording clues could not solve it.

### Development and blind scores were not calibrated

Early development scores appeared optimistic beside blind realism scores. They
came from different instruments and were never comparable. The Experiment
projection now namespaces coherent build, gameplay, accessibility, realism,
human play, and public release instead of treating them as one latent score.

### Accessibility arrived as a repair instead of an invariant

Two child rounds were spent separating editorial identification, exact visible
wording, objective non-text evidence, and player interpretation. The 0.12
contract makes this separation explicit, requires interpretation to remain
empty, and requires native and accessible channels to expose the same
load-bearing propositions.

### Capable players masked under-specified proof

Players inferred intended murder despite missing death evidence and bridged
ambiguous authorship. The process improved when it asked which exact released
record licensed each proposition. The new claim trace binds each accepted
proof-path member to a released resource hash, visible locus, authorized phase,
and proposition before the future human-play run.

### Whole-suite changes made requalification expensive

Candidate 4 regenerated 18 artifacts and Candidate 7 preflights changed 19.
Any artifact-byte change invalidates approval and realism evidence; a
player-visible proof change can invalidate gameplay. Shared capabilities should
first climb on one or two source-contracted representatives, then propagate
once.

### No human table has validated the party experience

Five development playtests used model players. They support logical
reachability, blindness, and channel parity. They do not establish whether six
humans can inhabit the roles for 150 minutes, enjoy the pacing, or experience a
satisfying resolution. That remains first-order work in Issues #15–#17.

## Supported and unsupported claims

Supported:

- Candidate 6 is the deterministic gameplay baseline.
- Two isolated model players solved through different evidence channels with
  no hints and 94% culprit confidence.
- Exact replay reproduced the Candidate 6 Release and Physical Export.
- Candidate 6 contains 19 independently measured artifacts.
- All 19 public Verismill Experiment replays verified during migration.
- Artifact realism improved between measured suites but remained unaccepted.

Unsupported:

- Candidate 6 artifacts passed Verismill realism acceptance.
- Candidate 6 is public-release qualified.
- Candidate 7 is an approved or independently measured child.
- Model playtests establish human enjoyment, immersion, or replay value.
- One overall score describes the game's quality.

## Adopted operating principles

1. One user-owned Workspace and Experiment is the game spine from concept
   approval onward.
2. Every selected rung exports a portable `.ngw`.
3. Current standing is a replaceable projection derived from journals.
4. Per-artifact Verismill buses remain authoritative and are referenced through
   verified public capsules, not private storage imports.
5. Claim traces precede blind human play and successful resolution requires a
   licensed released proof path, not only an answer-key match.
6. Accessibility is a first-order artifact invariant.
7. Shared renderer capabilities climb on representative source contracts before
   whole-suite propagation.
8. Failed hard gates override attractive aggregates.
9. Human feedback and scoped exact-hash approval remain first-order evidence.
10. Complete transcripts should be persisted from the first blind run.

## Recommended disposition

Keep Candidate 6 frozen as the gameplay baseline. Treat Candidate 7 as renderer
research until a bounded capability passes, impacted artifacts are rebuilt,
proof-affecting changes receive fresh gameplay, exact bytes receive human
approval, and new independent Verismill panels measure them.

In parallel, deepen the six dossiers and conduct the first exact-version
six-human playtest. Human feedback should govern party experience while model
panels remain cheaper adversarial evidence for logic and artifact defects.
