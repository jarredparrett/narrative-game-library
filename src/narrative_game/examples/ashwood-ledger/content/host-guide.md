# The Ashwood Ledger

## Host guide

You are facilitating a 60-75 minute archival mystery for two players. Keep this guide private.

### Premise

The trustees of the fictional Vale House Archive are preparing an estate inventory. Its handwritten property ledger vanished overnight. The missing entry concerns a 1997 transfer of 18 Quillstone Mews in Madison, New Jersey. The players must determine who removed the ledger, why it was removed, and which records corroborate their conclusion.

### Canonical resolution

Records clerk Noel Voss accepted an undeclared payment from a developer's intermediary. Voss then used the staff key to remove the ledger so the trustees would not discover the indexed 1997 deed before signing a redevelopment option. No one forced the records-room window.

The deed was executed on October 17, 1997. It states consideration of $425,000.00. These two document claims are authoritative only because they are pinned in the Artifact Forge request; all other story connections come from the game's canonical propositions.

### Run

- Opening, 15 minutes: players introduce their characters and inspect the opening records.
- Investigation, 25 minutes: deliver the private investigation envelopes and invite evidence sharing.
- Resolution, 15 minutes: deliver the deed envelope and ask for one hypothesis plus one acceptable proof path.
- Debrief, 10 minutes: read the resolution and show the claim-lineage table.

### Recovery

If the group stalls, ask: "Which record establishes physical access, which record rules out outside entry, and what transaction was someone trying to keep out of the inventory?"

### Safety and provenance

Every detached print asset must remain inside its labeled game container. The deed is a fictional game artifact generated for this scenario, not evidence of a real transaction and not legal advice.
